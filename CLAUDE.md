# Bat calls

Deep Learning class project (team of 3). A model listens to a call of an Egyptian fruit bat and predicts what the bats were doing: Biting, Feeding, Fighting, Mating protest or Sleeping.

The student driving this repo is new to deep learning and must be able to explain every line in class.

## How to work here

- Keep everything as simple as possible: three short scripts, no comments or docstrings in the code, no extra files or folders.
- One problem at a time. After a run, say in plain language what the problem is, offer 2-3 fixes with their trade-offs and a recommendation, then stop. **The student chooses the fix.** Don't apply fixes that weren't chosen, and don't combine several changes in one step.
- Explain in the chat, not in the code. Never fake results; say when a difference is too small to mean anything.
- Compare fixes on the cross-validation score only (one run of `train.py` takes about 13 minutes; it took 27 minutes with 4-second pictures and 72 minutes with ResNet50 on top, rows 17 and 18). The test days are used once, at the very end. (In the end they were looked at four times: rows 6, 7, 13 and 20 of the log. Any new fix must be judged on cross-validation, not on the test days.)
- Commit each chosen fix separately and push, then add a row to the log below.

## The pieces

- `download.py`: gets the labels and every recording of the 5 contexts from Figshare (Prat et al. 2017, https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2017.143, CC BY 4.0) into `data/audio/` (50,998 recordings, about 54 GB).
- `prepare.py`: first 2 seconds of each annotated call become a 128x256 spectrogram (0-62.5 kHz; shorter calls are filled up with silence). The last 10% of recording days are the test set (fold -1). The other 302 days are cut into 5 folds (0-4) of about 60 consecutive days each, so every fold is a block of time and a day is never split. Then Sleeping calls are removed at random (fixed seed) until there are as many as Fighting calls: 7,963 of 33,997 are kept, everywhere including the test days. That leaves 26,735 calls: 23,526 in the folds (2,324 to 6,558 per fold; the first fold has no Biting calls) and 3,209 test calls. Writes `data/x.npy` and `data/y.csv` (label, fold and the hour of the recording, 0-23).
- `train.py`: 5-fold cross-validation. For each fold a fresh ResNet18 (pretrained on ImageNet, all layers trained) is trained on the other 4 folds and checked on the held-out fold. In training, one random frequency band and one random time band (each up to 16 pixels) of every picture are set to silence; held-out calls are never blanked. Training takes 4 epochs: the learning rate is 1e-4 in epochs 1-3 and ten times smaller (1e-5) in epoch 4. The clock: the network's probabilities, which come from the sound alone, are multiplied by the share of each context's training calls that happen at the hour of the recording (`clock`, a 24x5 table counted from the training folds, plus 1 per cell so nothing is ruled out completely). The model gives its two most likely contexts per call. Prints train and validation accuracy per epoch, a per-context report and a confusion matrix (rows: true context, columns: first guess, both in the order of `CLASSES`) over all 5 held-out folds, and the accuracy with one guess and within two guesses next to the blind-guess numbers (always answering the most common context, or the two most common). `train.py test` skips the cross-validation: it trains one model on all 5 folds and checks it on the test days.

Up to row 7 of the log the folds mixed days from all months, so a held-out day had neighbouring days in training and the cross-validation score (0.720) was far above the test score (0.607). Since row 8 a held-out fold is a block of about 60 consecutive days, which is closer to the test days. Scores from row 8 on are not comparable with rows 0-7. Cross-validation is for comparing fixes, not a prediction of the test score.

Run with `.venv\Scripts\python <script>`. `data/` is not in git.

The test days (2013-05-17 to 2014-02-19, 34 days) come from the dataset's Treatment IDs 18-20, the folds almost entirely from IDs 1-17. IDs 18 and 19 are the same 10 bats as IDs 16 and 17 (the two largest training groups, recorded 2012-12-18 to 2013-05-11), mixed into two new groups on 2013-05-13 and recorded on the same channels; ID 20 is 6 of those bats in February 2014. So the test days are familiar bats and microphones, later in time and regrouped; they are not unfamiliar bats. Only the first fold (IDs 1-2, other bats, 432 of their calls left in training) comes close to checking unfamiliar bats. The student's stated goal (2026-11-14) is to classify "free bat data": calls from recordings the model has never heard.
